I am a Ph.D. candidate at Princeton University’s School of Public and International Affairs, specializing in International Relations and Security Studies. My research examines how international pressures shape the strategic choices of states and the resilience of their political institutions. I work across two connected areas: nuclear security and coercive diplomacy, and the international politics of democratic resilience.

**Nuclear security and coercive diplomacy.** My dissertation, *The Politics of Nuclear Prevention: Strategic Choices in Proliferation Crises*, examines why the United States and its allies choose diplomacy, sanctions, coercion, or military force when confronting states pursuing nuclear capabilities. Focusing on Iran, North Korea, Iraq, and Libya, the project combines original data and statistical analysis with archival research and process tracing. My archival research has included work at the UK National Archives, the British Library, the LSE Archives, and the Churchill Archives Centre.

**International politics and democratic resilience.** A second line of my research examines how international actors and pressures shape domestic political institutions. I am particularly interested in how great-power coercion, sanctions, international organizations, intervention, and international legal institutions affect the survival, transformation, and breakdown of democratic institutions and practices. This agenda builds on my earlier research on international organizations, statebuilding, democratic institutions, and international law, and extends my broader interest in how international forces reshape domestic political order.

Across these areas, I combine quantitative and qualitative methods, including original data collection, statistical analysis, archival research, process tracing, and comparative case studies. My work speaks to broader debates about nuclear proliferation, coercive diplomacy, international institutions, the use of force, and democratic resilience.

My research has been presented at Uppsala University’s Alva Myrdal Centre for Nuclear Disarmament, the Midwest Political Science Association Annual Conference, Princeton’s Center for International Security Studies, Yale’s MacMillan Center, and the University of Chicago’s International Relations Conference. During the 2025–26 academic year, I was a Resident Fellow in Yale’s Nuclear Security Program and was previously a Graduate Fellow at Princeton’s Center for International Security Studies. My research has been supported by Princeton PIIRS, the Princeton School of Public and International Affairs, and the Liechtenstein Institute on Self-Determination, among others.
