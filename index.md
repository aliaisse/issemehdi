I am a Ph.D. candidate in Security Studies & International Relations at Princeton University's School of Public and International Affairs. My research sits at the intersection of nuclear strategy, coercive diplomacy, and grand strategy, with a focus on how states respond to the most consequential security challenges of our time.

My research integrates quantitative and qualitative methods to study nuclear security. My work includes building original datasets, regression analysis, and theory-driven comparisons to identify patterns and explain strategic decision-making. I am particularly interested in drawing credible inferences from imperfect data, using small-N designs, careful case selection, and empirically grounded research strategies. 

My work has been presented at Uppsala University's Alva Myrdal Center for Nuclear Disarmament, the Midwest Political Science Association Annual Conference, Princeton's Center for International Security Studies, Yale's MacMillan Center for International and Area Studies, and University of Chicago's International Relations Conference, among other venues. I was a Graduate Fellow at Princeton’s Center for International Security Studies (2022–2025). I have received support from Princeton PIIRS, the Princeton School of Public and International Affairs, and the Liechtenstein Institute on Self-Determination, among others.

During the 2025–26 academic year, I was a Resident Fellow in the Nuclear Security Program at Yale University’s MacMillan Center. My dissertation examines counterproliferation strategy and proliferation crises. During Summer 2025, I conducted archival research at the British National Archives, the British Library, LSE Archives, and the Churchill Archives Centre. I am on the academic job market for 2026–27. 

I hold an M.A. in Public Affairs from Princeton University and an M.A. in Social Science (Political Science & International Relations) from the University of Chicago, where I was awarded the Maroon Scholar Research Award. I also completed advanced coursework in international law, focusing on the use of force and the law of armed conflict, at the University of Cambridge.

For more information about my research, teaching, or service, please feel free to contact me.

Thank you!
